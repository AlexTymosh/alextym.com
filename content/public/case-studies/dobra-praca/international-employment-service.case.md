# End-to-End International Employment Service

## Overview

- This case demonstrates The Owner's experience in customer journey mapping, service
  design, knowledge management, process analysis, customer self-service, workflow
  coordination, and operational risk reduction.

## Context

- The Owner designed a coordinated end-to-end service for customers preparing to work
  abroad.
- The service covered the customer journey from initial consultation and document
  preparation through travel, arrival, accommodation, and access to support in the
  destination country.
- Customers received assistance with employment documentation, insurance, visa
  preparation, transport arrangements, job confirmation, and accommodation coordination.

## Implementation — Coordinated Service

- International journeys could require two or three forms of transport, so the company
  coordinated tickets and instructions for each stage of the route.
- Customers received stage-specific instructions explaining what to do, where to go,
  which documents to present, and whom to contact.
- Customers were provided with simple vocabulary cards for initial conversations in the
  destination country.
- Where available, customers received a SIM card from a foreign mobile operator before
  leaving Ukraine.
- The preparation package included the contact details of the local coordinator, a
  customer-support hotline, and the relevant consular contact.
- Customers also received a contingency plan for common travel, documentation,
  communication, or arrival problems.

## Problem

- The Owner identified that employees were losing substantial time answering the same
  customer questions repeatedly at different stages of the process.
- The analysis showed that customers frequently forgot previously provided information
  and repeated the same questions across several service stages.

## Analysis

- To analyse the problem, the Owner created a question map for one customer journey.
- Employees marked the subject category whenever the customer asked a new question.

## Implementation — Knowledge and Preparation

- The Owner responded by creating a customer roadmap that defined which subjects should
  be discussed at each stage.
- Frequently asked questions and general preparation guidance were also published on the
  company website.
- Customers could complete an online preparation questionnaire based on the supplied
  guidance.
- Customers who demonstrated that they had reviewed the preparation materials received a
  service incentive in the form of priority processing.

## Results

- The redesigned information flow reduced repeated consultations and made the customer
  journey more predictable.
- The service combined human support, self-service information, structured instructions,
  external-provider coordination, and contingency planning.

## Limitations

- The questionnaire encouraged customers to prepare independently while still retaining
  access to personal support for exceptional or sensitive questions.
