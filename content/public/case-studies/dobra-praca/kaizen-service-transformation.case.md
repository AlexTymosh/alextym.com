# Kaizen-Driven Service Delivery Transformation

## Overview

- This case demonstrates The Owner's experience in business analysis, Lean process
  improvement, service design, customer research, workflow segmentation, automation,
  quality management, Gemba, customer self-service, operational scaling, and
  data-supported decision-making.

## Context

- While studying business and management concepts at university, the Owner learned about
  Toyota's Kaizen philosophy and began applying continuous-improvement principles to
  Dobra Praca.
- The main transformation began around 2015–2016 and focused on making international
  employment services faster, simpler, safer, and more accessible for customers.
- The Ukrainian employment market had changed rapidly because of political disruption,
  territorial changes, the beginning of the war, and large-scale internal displacement.
- Many customers were seeking employment abroad under financial and personal pressure
  and wanted to complete the process as quickly as possible.

## Problem

- The findings showed that the traditional recruitment model required substantial
  redesign rather than minor administrative improvements.
- The Owner analysed delays in recruitment, candidate assessment, document processing,
  communication, and office-based service delivery.
- The previous service relied heavily on office visits, manual interviews, repeated
  document checks, and knowledge held by individual employees.

## Analysis

- The Owner conducted market and customer research that identified three important
  factors: speed, fear of moving to an unfamiliar country, and pressure created by
  difficult personal circumstances.
- He used Lean principles to remove repeated work, unnecessary visits, duplicated data
  entry, waiting periods, and activities that did not contribute directly to the
  customer's final outcome.

## Implementation — Customer Journey and Self-Service

- The Owner redesigned the customer journey so applicants could submit initial forms and
  preliminary information through an online portal.
- Self-service reduced manual data entry and allowed customers to begin the process
  without travelling to the office.
- Mandatory office attendance was reduced to two essential stages: formal submission of
  visa-related documents and collection of completed documents.

## Implementation — Work Segmentation and Escalation

- The Owner divided work according to complexity rather than assigning every case to the
  same level of specialist.
- Standardised, lower-risk tasks such as initial validation, completeness checks, and
  basic vacancy matching were delegated to junior employees, students, or external
  agents.
- Complex, valuable, or unusual cases were escalated to experienced specialists with
  stronger recruitment, sales, communication, and negotiation skills.
- This allowed senior employees to focus on cases requiring judgement, deeper HR
  knowledge, individual support, or resolution of exceptional circumstances.
- Customers who indicated specialist experience or a need for individual assistance were
  flagged for direct follow-up by a more experienced employee.
- The Owner introduced an agent model that allowed customers and external participants
  to refer and support additional candidates.
- The company retained control over final validation, quality, documentation,
  contractual relationships, and delivery while distributing simpler acquisition and
  support activities across a wider network.

## Implementation — Communication and Gemba

- The Owner introduced IP telephony to improve international communication and reduce
  customer uncertainty.
- Customers could speak directly with coordinators or representatives located in the
  destination country before travelling.
- A customer-support line was established in another country.
- The company negotiated agreements with telecommunications providers and could provide
  customers with a foreign mobile SIM card before departure from Ukraine.
- The Owner applied the Kaizen principle of Gemba by personally visiting workplaces and
  accommodation in different countries.
- He inspected employment locations, working conditions, and accommodation and collected
  photographs that could be shown to future customers.
- Customers were encouraged and rewarded for providing photographs and practical
  feedback from their workplaces.
- These measures reduced uncertainty and gave future applicants more direct evidence
  about expected working and living conditions.

## Implementation — Quality and Standardisation

- The Owner introduced employee incentives for identifying, documenting, and reporting
  process errors rather than concealing them.
- Recorded errors were treated as input for process improvement and prevention, not only
  as individual performance failures.
- The Owner replaced long written instructions and extended onboarding with short
  process diagrams, checklists, and practical workflow demonstrations.
- Internal and international recruitment processes were separated and standardised.
- Procedures were represented through flowcharts, allowing employees to guide customers
  through clearly defined stages.
- The company signed agreements with passenger transport providers to connect
  recruitment, documentation, travel, and arrival support.

## Implementation — Scaling and Remote Access

- The Owner introduced a group-based recruitment format for periods of unusually high
  demand.
- Instead of repeating the same introductory explanations in many individual meetings,
  candidates attended a shared information session in a large room.
- Common questions were answered collectively, while personal or complex cases continued
  separately with a recruiter or with the Owner.
- This model was developed for an unusual market situation in which overseas employers
  had high and sometimes effectively open demand for workers.
- The Owner also focused on customers in rural or remote areas who found it difficult to
  identify trustworthy employment providers or travel repeatedly to a regional centre.
- The company used targeted communication distributed with local utility bills and
  provided initial online contact and document pre-processing.
- This allowed remote customers to complete preliminary stages before visiting the
  office and removed at least one unnecessary trip to the regional centre.

## Implementation — Risk and Service Controls

- The Owner applied risk-analysis principles so the company committed limited resources
  before a customer formally entered the service process.
- Risk controls included repeated confirmation procedures, involvement of a trusted
  relative where appropriate, and maintaining a broader contact network for the
  customer.
- The Owner introduced a deferred-payment service model designed to reduce the upfront
  financial barrier for customers who could not afford the full cost before starting
  work abroad.
- The service included a commitment to continue supporting the customer through the
  overseas placement process rather than ending support after the initial application
  stage.

## Results

- At its peak, the company exceeded 200 fully supported customer placements in one
  month.
- The operating model combined self-service, standardisation, distributed agents,
  junior-level processing, specialist escalation, direct international communication,
  Gemba inspections, quality controls, and continuous improvement.
- The redesigned service reduced reliance on expensive specialists for routine work and
  allowed the company to support more customers without proportional growth in fixed
  operating costs.
- The model improved scalability because agents and junior staff could perform
  standardised stages while experienced employees retained control over complex cases
  and final outcomes.
- By 2018, Dobra Praca achieved first place in its regional sector by financial results.

## Limitations

- The regional result cannot be attributed to one change alone, but the Kaizen-driven
  operating model, workflow standardisation, automation, service accessibility, and
  scalable delivery structure contributed to the company's development.
